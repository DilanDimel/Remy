Transcript reading study (Dilan, Nov 28: "a deep test like the resumes, 10,000s of them, unique, different"). Server only, so students and owners on the website and the app get it through `POST /api/import/profile`.

- `script/transcript-check.ts` (`npm run check:transcript`): 12 registrar layouts x 4 grade scales plus Parchment covers, keys, AP/CLEP/IB, study abroad, repeats, withdrawals, standing notes and short pages. `TRANSCRIPT_CASES=1000` = 12,000 PDFs, 11,194 different formats, all pass (about 2 min). 43 real PDFs found online were read locally (never committed); 12 of them were scans.
- Found and fixed: `dropRunningLines` (`server/resume/parse.ts`) took two course rows that only differ in numbers, one ending a page and one starting the next, for a running header and deleted both. Headers and footers are now counted separately (resume reading too; `check:resume` passes).
- Reader (`server/resume/transcript.ts`): window, cover/key trimming, scans read by `scanText(buf, "transcript")` (it used the resume prompt, which answered NOT_A_RESUME), prompt for several labeled GPAs, out-of-100 averages, narrative schools, every course, in-progress courses, no probation notes; up to 30 facts and 8000 tokens. `transcriptGrounded` lets a course list or a joined quote pass the quote check when every course code sits on a printed line with its grade.
- Real reader (Claude Haiku 5.5, the students' model) on 360 transcripts: courses found 83% before, 99.5% after; school, majors, credits, labeled GPAs, honors, withdrawn courses and standing notes at or near 100%. About $0.0014 a transcript; the whole study spent about $1.70.
